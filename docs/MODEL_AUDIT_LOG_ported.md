**P0.2 Seawall as a crest barrier (M2).**
- **Mechanism:** `reflective_barrier`, which multiplied wave and level by (1 − Kr²), is replaced by a new effect kind, **`crest_barrier`**:
  - Instance params: `crestM` (m above MSL, default 2.5, placeholder) and `frontageShare` (0–1, default 1.0, placeholder).
  - **Index model:** the still-water level at the shoreline in metres is built from the same parts as the map's water level:

    WL = tide + L·surge_ref + F·stage_ref, capped in the compound case.

    - If WL ≤ crest, the wall is **held**: wave and level × (1 − frontageShare), so it never cuts more than the frontage it covers.
    - If WL > crest, it is **overtopped**: no protection, flagged.
  - **Map extent:** barriers act physically (`barrierCrests` in `inundation.ts`).
    - A barrier covers `frontageShare` of the segment's shore cells, most sea-exposed first.
    - Water crossing from the water onto a covered cell is blocked while its level is ≤ the crest; the uncovered frontage still lets water in (flanking).
    - To avoid counting a barrier twice, the map takes the index model's transmission *before* the barrier pass (`transmissionPreBarrier`).
  - The reflection coefficient stays only as a **toe-scour / beach-loss flag** on sandy segments. A **coastal-squeeze flag** is added where a barrier fronts mangroves.
- **Citation added:** `eurotop2018`. Goda 2010 and USACE CEM are kept for the reflection note.

**P0.3 Dune as a crest barrier (M3).**
- **Mechanism:** the same `crest_barrier`, after the FEMA 540 ft² (50.2 m²) erosion test. A dune that fails the test is removed for the event.
- **Removed:** `protectionWhenRetained` (a flat 35%, placeholder, no source) is deleted.
- **New parameter:** `crestM`, default 3.0 m, placeholder.

## Phase 1: one water level in metres (`feature/model-p1`, cut from `feature/model-p0`)

### What changed

**P1.1 One water level in metres (M11).**
- **Formula.** At every water cell:

  WL = MHHW + SLR + surge(RP) + river(x, RP) − mangrove − storage, where surge(RP) = storm tide(RP) − MHHW.

  - **Deviation from the brief's formula, on purpose.** The brief writes MHHW + SLR + η_storm(RP). The CoDEC storm-tide levels are total water levels: they already contain the tide. Adding MHHW on top would count the tide twice. So the storm part is the storm tide minus MHHW, and WL = storm tide + SLR when there is no river.
  - **MHHW still matters.** It is the level the river flood rides on. It sets the "wet at every high tide" class. It also caps what mangroves can remove: they act only on the surge above MHHW.
- **The fill.** The level spreads over connected land; depth = WL − ground.
  - **Barriers** (seawalls, dunes) and **khazan bunds** block water up to their crest.
  - **One fill feeds both** the map and the per-segment tables. Segment results are the segment's part of the same extent, so the two cannot disagree. A test checks this: segment sums equal the extent, and the depth map is exactly the flooded cells.
- **Mangroves** act in cm/km × width directly (McIvor et al. 2012b), in metres, with no reference-surge division.
  - **The existing belts are thin.** The CRZ grid gives 54 m (riverfront) and 98 m (St Inez), so at 26 cm/km they lower the surge by only 1–3 cm. That is too little to change any ~55 m cell.
  - A 300 m belt added on the riverfront lowers its shoreline level by 7.8 cm.
- **Storage** (khazan polder, check dam) subtracts its volume from the river water on the segment's low-lying land (river stage × area), and lowers the river part in proportion.
- **Waves** are no longer added to the hazard. The open-coast wave indicator (share of open-sea wave energy) is reported in the segment working for Miramar and Dona Paula only. Breakwaters and coastal vegetation act on it.
- **Removed with the index:**
  - **Index terms:** H0, H, V, α (`waveWeight`), `referenceSurgeM`, `referenceFloodDepthM`, `referenceFloodStageM` (replaced by river stage per RP) and `lambdaLevelPerKm`.
    - The storm tide is now carried flat over the estuary. Sundar & Shetye (2005) find the tide amplifying up the Mandovi, so a decay was the wrong direction anyway.
  - **`susceptibility`**, removed here rather than in P2.2, because the metres model has no term it could multiply.
  - **`compoundCap`** stays in the data file but is not applied. The old index never reached it (L + F ≤ 2 < 3). P2.6 removes it after the timing check.
- **Segment tables** now show:
  - the water level at the shoreline (m);
  - newly flooded and behind-bund km²;
  - exposure inside the flooded land.

  "Show your work" lists every term in metres.

**P1.2 Return periods (M5).**
- **Storm tide comes from CoDEC (Muis et al. 2020), not COAST-RP.**
  - **Why:** COAST-RP (Dullaart et al. 2021, 4TU.ResearchData) was unreachable. The 4TU host timed out on TCP (20 s, several tries on 22 Oct 2026). The figshare mirror's ndownloader returned 504s.
  - **What CoDEC is:** the same group's GTSM-ERA5 Gumbel return levels, Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.3660927, CC BY 4.0.
  - **Extraction:** `tools/build_storm_tide.py` takes the GTSM output point nearest the Mandovi mouth (73.790 E, 15.485 N): `id_coast_glob_12128` at 73.7549 E, 15.5127 N, 4.9 km away. It writes the point's ID, coordinates and Gumbel parameters into `hazards.json` (`stormTideByRp`).

  | RP | Storm tide (m above MSL) | Note |
  |---|---|---|
  | ~1 yr | 1.119 | Gumbel location (mode); CoDEC has no RP 1 (a Gumbel return period must exceed 1 yr, the mode is ~1.58 yr) |
  | 10 yr | 1.206 | |
  | 50 yr | 1.270 | |
  | 100 yr | 1.297 | |

  - **Spread between nearby points.** The next two points, 42 km SE and 54 km NW, give 1.252 and 1.314 m at 100 yr.
  - **Tagged `reasoned_extrapolation`, not solid:**
    1. ERA5 (~31 km) under-represents tropical cyclones, so cyclone storm tides are likely **under-stated**. This is why the brief preferred COAST-RP.
    2. No wave setup.
    3. MSL is assumed equal to EGM2008, as for the tide.
  - **These levels are much lower than Phase 0's placeholder.** Phase 0 put tide 0.6 + reference surge 1.5 = 2.1 m at the open coast; CoDEC gives 1.30 m at 100 yr. Most of the drop in storm-tide flooding below comes from this.
  - **No range on the storm tide.** CoDEC gives one value per return period. The uncertainty is open: COAST-RP, or a cyclone-resolving study.
- **Cross-check against PSMSL 1249 could not be done.**
  - **PSMSL:** only the monthly RLR series is public (`https://psmsl.org/data/obtaining/rlr.monthly.data/1249.rlrdata`, 660 months from 1969). Monthly means cannot give annual maxima.
  - **UHSLC:** no hourly Mormugao record. `meta.geojson` lists 600 stations; on India's west coast only Cochin and Minicoy, both fast-delivery only.
  - **Left open:** an hourly Mormugao series (Survey of India / INCOIS).
- **River stage stays a placeholder.**
  - **No data source reachable:** CWC Goa stage records are not published online in a usable form. GloFAS reanalysis needs a Copernicus EWDS account, which this pass does not have.
  - **Values:**
    - RP 10 keeps Phase 0's reference stage, 1.0 m (0.5–2.0).
    - The other return periods scale it by judgement: RP 1 0.5 m (0.25–1.0), RP 50 1.3 m (0.65–2.6), RP 100 1.5 m (0.75–3.0).
    - All are `placeholder_needs_calibration`. They are not data; the note says so.
- **Compound:** both the storm tide and the river stage are taken at the same return period (an assumption, stated in the UI). P2.6 checks the joint timing.
- **UI:** a return-period selector next to the hazard layer (not shown for the high-tide + SLR layer). The Methodology panel lists the RP table.

**P1.4 Terrain (M8).**

The tiles come only as per-continent zips (Asia.zip ≈ 17.3 GB) or via Earth Engine (needs an account). Its GitHub mirror request (portolan-sdi/portolan-data#46) is open, with no mirror yet.
- **What is in place:**

**P1.5 Bunds and three wet classes (M9).**
- **No bund lines from the data.**
  - **Sluice candidates:** `existingDefenses.json` has 17 candidate sluice points, not lines.
  - **CZMP:** the bund symbology was not extracted, because the legend is missing.
- **Fallback: a khazan-protected mask, as the brief allows.** Every khazan cell (CZMP khazan mask) is behind a bund, and the bund line is the mask edge. Water entering khazan land from outside is held while its level is ≤ the bund crest.
  - **Crest:** 1.5 m (range 1.0–2.0), `placeholder_needs_calibration`. It is set above spring high water (+1.0 m) because the bunds are known to keep out normal tides; it is not a measured height.
- **Three classes**, each reported on its own:

  | Class | What it is | Counted? |
  |---|---|---|
  | Wet at every high tide | Below today's central MHHW and connected, computed once | No (0.98 km² in scope) |
  | Behind bunds | Khazan land the event floods | Reported separately |
  | Newly flooded | All other flooded land | The headline |

  - **The range is monotonic again.** Because "wet at every high tide" is fixed at today's central MHHW, the newly-flooded extent no longer depends on the tide the way it did in Phase 0. The tide envelope (`hazardBoundCandidates`) is removed, and `tests/p0.test.ts` still checks that the displayed range covers every one-at-a-time sweep.
- **Bund failure** is a toggle in the Controls rail.
  - **Effect:** compound RP 1 goes from 0.56 to 1.01 km² newly flooded, and 0 to 0.13 km² behind bunds. Water that gets past failed bunds reaches non-khazan land behind them.
  - **Why the effect is small at high tide:** only ~0.04 km² of khazan sits below ~1.1 m on GLO-30. The DSM's canopy and bund heights probably raise the khazan surface; DeltaDTM would test that.

**P1.6 Overland attenuation.**
- **Range:** 0–1.0 m/km, default 0, from Vafeidis et al. (2019). They test 0–100 cm/km: wetland studies report up to 70 cm/km, and 100 cm/km is their expert upper limit. Checked at the paper.
- **Scope:** it applies to the storm-driven part only. The tide, SLR and river stage are not attenuated.
- **In the range:** the least favourable bound uses 0.
- **Tag:** `reasoned_extrapolation` (a global sensitivity range, not a Panjim value).

### Sensitivity (P1)

**How it was run:**
- **Measure:** one input at a time, everything else central, existing measures in place.
- **Output:** newly flooded km² in the Panjim scope, from `npx vite-node tools/sensitivity.ts`.
- **Displayed range:** the full bound run (all inputs, the SLR likely range and the solution ranges together).

Brief: `docs/STEP_PROMPT_deltadtm_switch.md`. Ferdin supplied the DeltaDTM tile that 4TU would not serve; this pass masks it, makes it the default, and re-pins the regression. It closes P1.4's open item ("no DeltaDTM differences logged").

### What changed

**The grid** (`node tools/build_dem_grid.mjs --source deltadtm data/source/deltadtm/DeltaDTM_Mandovi_EPSG4326.tif` → `src/data/panjimDemGrid.deltadtm.json`).

That is two resamplings before the builder's own averaging to ~55 m cells; the grid's source note now says so. The tiles stay in `data/source/deltadtm/` (untracked, 4 MB); the built grid is committed.

**P2.3 Metric chain (M13).**
- **Hazard:** depth, extent (km²; the brief says hectares, the UI keeps km² for consistency with every existing table, and the ecology card uses ha), and **annual chance** shown with every return period (~63%, 10%, 2%, 1% a year).
- **Exposure:** CRZ-II and CRZ-III land, **people** and **buildings** inside the flooded land (new exposure categories), facilities.
- **Impact** (`src/core/impact.ts`): damage in ₹, **people affected** (people in cells deeper than 0.15 m; range 0.10–0.30 m, editable in Model assumptions, `reasoned_extrapolation`: the audit's own convention), buildings in flooded land, facilities wet.
- **Risk:** **expected annual damage** EAD = Σ ½ (D_k + D_k+1)(p_k − p_k+1), p = 1/RP over RP 1, 10, 50, 100 (`runRisk`), and people affected per year the same way.
  - Two caveats, both in the UI note: events rarer than 100 years are left out (a lower bound); and "RP 1" is the Gumbel mode, whose annual chance is 0.63, not 1, so the frequent end leans high.
- **Benefit of a measure:** EAD with today's measures − EAD with today's measures plus that one measure (central, per added measure), in the Test panel, plus the total benefit of all changes with its range.
- **Equity:** EAD and people per year grouped by SVI class.
- **Damage only on new flooding:** newly flooded and behind-bund cells carry damage; land wet at every high tide does not.

**P2.4 Ecology (M14).**
- **Mangrove and khazan are no longer exposure.** `khazanKm2` and `mangroveKm2` leave the exposure categories, totals and the Explore list. They move to a separate **Ecology (not damage)** card (`src/core/ecology.ts`, `runEcology`), never added to damage.
- **Mangrove squeeze:** for each SLR horizon (2050, 2100, at the chosen SSP), mangrove with no *new* tidal land within 200 m to move into. New tidal land = dry at today's MHHW but reached by MHHW + SLR over connected land (seawalls, dunes and khazan bunds hold it back up to their crest), not CRZ-II urban, under 20% roofs and roads. Migration distance and the 20% are placeholders (`src/data/ecology.json`). Sources for the concept: Schuerch et al. (2018), Pontee (2013).
  - **Result (existing measures):** 2050 SSP2-4.5 (+0.19 m): 201 of 204 ha squeezed, 1.5 ha of new tidal land within reach. 2100 SSP2-4.5 (+0.51 m): 186 ha, 18.1 ha. 2100 SSP5-8.5 (+0.75 m): 184 ha, 24.3 ha.
  - **Read with care.** The surface DEM puts the land behind the mangroves (median 1.5 m) and the canopy itself (median 2.2 m) above the ground, so new tidal land is under-read and nearly every stand looks squeezed. A seawall on the riverfront (crest 3 m) did not change the numbers, because so little new tidal land exists there on this DEM. The indicator is wired to respond to barriers, roads and urban land (tested: a seawall never reduces the squeeze), but the DEM is the binding limit.
  - I first counted all land within the future tidal frame as migration space; that made the squeeze *fall* with sea-level rise and said 98% of the mangrove is squeezed *today*. It now counts only new tidal land, and is not defined with no rise.
- **Khazan:** "khazan flooded" is replaced by **khazan flooded over intact bunds** (overtopping) and **khazan flooded if the bunds failed** (failure exposure), per event. Examples: river flood RP 1: 0 ha over the bunds, 3.9 ha if they fail; RP 100: 34.1 and 34.7 ha; compound RP 100: 62.6 ha either way (the water tops the bunds).
- **Mangrove-lined creeks** report their new habitat (ha) on the card.

**P2.5 Rain at high tide (M15).** A new layer, "Heavy rain at high tide" (`src/core/rain.ts`).
- **Catchments:** a priority-flood over the DEM from four outlets: Ourem creek (OSM ways 89208051, 1411085644, 1156762035, 1427988789, 1340234669), Mala lake (89208031, 89208050, 89208039), St Inez creek (838100155, 1323666992, 1379887491, 1379887490), and the Mandovi along the riverfront for central Panjim. The creeks are narrower than a cell and are not water in the CRZ grid, so their lines are drawn in as outlets. Areas: central Panjim 0.97 km², Ourem 11.83 km², Mala 0.36 km², St Inez 4.98 km². Ourem is large because the Rio de Ourém channel runs south through the Taleigao fields.
- **Volume balance, per catchment, for one burst of rain centred on high tide:**
  - the tide is a semi-diurnal sine (12.42 h) with amplitude = MHHW; the outfall is closed while the tide stands above the outfall level (placeholder 0.3 m, range 0.0–0.6 m: city engineers say the outfalls are submerged at high tide);
  - runoff = rain × Σ (C_imp × built share + C_perv × unbuilt share) × cell area; C_imp 0.80 (0.70–0.87), C_perv 0.25 (0.10–0.40), McCuen (2017) via the Minnesota Stormwater Manual;
  - stored = runoff − drained while the outfall is open (placeholder 25 mm/h over the catchment, 15–50) − pumped − retained;
  - the stored volume ponds in the catchment's lowest connected land (level h with Σ(h − ground) × area = stored).
- **Rainfall: IMD 0.25° gridded daily rainfall** (Pai et al. 2014), not station return periods (not published openly; the 2026 Springer IDF study for Panaji is paywalled). `tools/build_rain_rp.py` downloads 1975–2024, keeps the cell centred 15.50 N 73.75 E (which holds Panjim), and fits a Gumbel by L-moments to the 50 annual maxima; range = 90% bootstrap interval. `reasoned_extrapolation`: a cell averages over ~27 km, so it under-states the point peak.

  | RP | 1-day rainfall (mm) | 90% interval |
  |---|---|---|
  | ~1 yr (Gumbel mode) | 160 | 151–171 |
  | 10 | 266 | 237–293 |
  | 50 | 343 | 297–386 |
  | 100 | 376 | 322–425 |

  Mean annual maximum 187 mm; largest 377 mm. The burst length (6 h, 3–12) is a placeholder.
- **Backflow and the "wet at every high tide" class.** Without a flap gate, tide water backs up the drains to MHHW + SLR over the catchment's land below it, and the rain ponds on top. Land that the tide reaches this way at an ordinary high tide (same drains, no rain, no SLR) counts as wet at every high tide, like land the sea reaches over the surface; only the event's extra ponding is new flooding. (My first run counted the everyday backflow as new flooding; it put 1.6 km² under water at RP 1.)
- **New measures** (`solutions.json`, effect kinds in `effects.ts`), each with a catchment selector (or the catchment under its map point): retention basin (m³ × share free at the start), stormwater pumping station (m³/s × availability), creek desilting/widening (added m³/s while open), flap/sluice gates (no backflow), mangrove-lined creek (added m³/s + habitat ha). They act only on this layer.
- **Mala, existing:** pumping station, Mala lake as retention, and two tide gates, from the press (Gomantak Times, 22 Jun 2023; Goa DIP press release, 5 Jul 2023).
  - Pumps: 3 × 300 HP + 2 × 60 HP = 1,020 HP = 761 kW; capacity = 0.75 × 761 kW / (ρ g × 5 m) = **11.6 m³/s**, a placeholder (lift and efficiency assumed; a 3–8 m lift gives 7–19 m³/s).
  - Retention: lake area 16,200 m² (OSM) × 1.5 m usable depth (assumed) = 24,000 m³.
  - **Cost discrepancy logged:** the Gomantak Times quotes "Rs 170 crore"; the state press release says about Rs 15.90 crore. The tool uses no cost.
  - Result: the Mala catchment stores nothing even at RP 100, consistent with the city's claim that Mala would stay dry.
- **Calibration note (sanity check, not calibration).** City engineers report ~15–20 rain-at-high-tide days a monsoon. The model ponds runoff in every catchment except Mala at the ~1-year rainfall (1.08 km² newly flooded), so it would flag such days as flooding: in the right direction, not testable further without event records.
- **Acceptance check against the map: NOT MET on the current DEM.** The known spots (18th June Road, Patto, the Kadamba bus stand) do not flood on GLO-30 at any return period; only single cells along Ourem creek do (`docs/model-audit/p2/map-rain-rp1-hotspots.png`, `map-rain-rp100-hotspots.png`). The rain ponds in the low fields of the Ourem and St Inez valleys instead (`map-rain-rp100-overview.png`).
  - **Why:** GLO-30 is a surface model. It puts central Panjim on rooftops (7–11 m around 18th June Road and Church Square) and Patto at 2–6 m.
  - **Checked on DeltaDTM** (the untracked grid in the working tree, comparison only, not switched): within ~110 m, Patto ponds up to 0.99 m (RP 1) and 1.25 m (RP 100), the bus stand 0.13 and 0.39 m. 18th June Road still does not: DeltaDTM also puts it at 7–9 m, so its flooding is local drainage (blocked drains, road crown) below this model's resolution.
  - So this acceptance item waits on the DeltaDTM switch (`docs/STEP_PROMPT_deltadtm_switch.md`), and 18th June Road on a drain-level model.

**The general rule for the rest.**

- **Sorting the land.** A spring-tide fill (no SLR, bunds intact) is run next to the MHHW fill. Any in-scope cell the spring tide reaches, but the normal high tide does not, is sorted three ways:
  - **Mangrove (CRZ-IA), ponds, and creek gaps the water flows through:** spring tides reach them as tidal water or habitat. They join "wet at every high tide" and are not counted.
  - **Everything else, outside the bunds:** "low land kept dry by defences the data doesn't have". 86 cells (0.25 km²) with the boxes; 61 cells (0.18 km²) once the St Inez / Merces fields are bunded. All unclassified land.
- **The defended class.** It is held like a bund at its own crest, the **1-year storm tide (1.119 m, CoDEC's Gumbel mode)**.
  - It is never newly flooded at RP 1.
  - At rarer events, it floods (and takes damage) only where the water exceeds spring high water by more than at RP 1.
  - It fails with the bund-failure toggle.
  - Rain that falls on it does count.
  - While dry, it is reported as its own line under the headline and hatched on the map (toggle in Controls → Map layers).
- **Same rule everywhere.** The unmapped defences also hold in the high tide + SLR fill and the mangrove-squeeze fill, so one rule governs every layer. See the consequences below.
- **Calibration test** (`tests/p3.test.ts`): a spring high tide with no storm and no rain floods 0 km² of new land, with ₹0 damage. Without the general rule (field bunds only) it flooded 0.49 km².

### A4: rain lands where it falls

**Model** (`src/core/rain.ts`).
- **Fill-and-spill:** each cell keeps its own share of the catchment's stored water (its share of the runoff) and passes it downhill along its priority-flood spill path.
- **Local hollows:** connected land of one catchment filled to the same spill level, above its ground. Each fills to its rim first and passes the rest on from its spill point.
- **Outlet sub-catchments:** water that reaches the creek or river ponds in its own outlet sub-catchment.
  - The outlet cells are grouped in order along each creek line (west to east along the Mandovi bank for central Panjim) into sub-catchments of at most about 1 km² of contributing land (`rain.subCatchmentKm2`, `placeholder_needs_calibration`).
  - Each ponds in its own lowest connected land, with the tide's backflow, as before.
- **Before:** the whole catchment's volume ponded at one level in its lowest land.
- **Deviation: no drain lines.** OSM maps only 6 `waterway=drain` ways in the window, too few to cut catchments at real outfalls, so the split is by contributing area along the creek. The brief's "D8 with a threshold" is replaced by the spill tree of the existing priority-flood.
- **Hollows only below 10 m.** Only hollows whose rim is at or below 10 m store water. Above that (DeltaDTM's domain ends at 10 m), hollows are artefacts: the first run found hollows on ground up to 76 m and up to 30 m deep, where DeltaDTM's lower values meet the GLO-30 fallback. Water runs on through them.
